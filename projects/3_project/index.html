SPRING Hospital Memory Clinic Robot

Multi-party conversational robot using LLMs deployed in a real hospital memory clinic.

Overview

As part of the EU-funded SPRING project, I integrated my PhD research into a real hospital memory clinic, deploying a multi-party conversational robot that interacts with patients, their companions, and clinicians. Eight research institutes collaborated on this project to tackle the challenges of social robots in complex healthcare environments.

This work was featured in TIME Magazine and won the EACL Best Demo Award in 2024.

Problem

Clinicians in memory clinics juggle patient wellbeing, carer input, and time pressure. Traditional conversational assistants fail to manage overlapping speech, interruptions, and sensitive topics in clinical settings. Most importantly, existing systems expect one-on-one conversations, but patients typically bring a carer or family member along.

Approach

Multi-party Dialogue Management: Built an LLM-powered dialogue manager that could track multiple speakers, repair interruptions, and adapt language for cognitive impairments. Unlike today’s voice assistants that expect to chat with one person at a time (like Alexa), this system naturally handles conversations with patients and their companions simultaneously.

Speech Diarisation & Intent Understanding: Combined speech diarisation, intent understanding, and safety constraints to keep the robot’s contributions concise and supportive in a clinical environment.

Clinical Collaboration: Worked directly with hospital staff to script handovers, escalation paths, and accessibility safeguards before live deployment, ensuring the system met real-world clinical requirements.

Key Contributions

This project demonstrated that LLM-powered social robots can be successfully deployed in sensitive healthcare environments when designed with accessibility and multi-party interaction as core requirements. The work informed broader best practices for deploying LLMs in safety-critical, accessibility-first environments and showed the importance of designing AI systems that consider cognitive diversity from the ground up.
