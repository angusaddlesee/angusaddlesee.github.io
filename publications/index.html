2025

  1. Socially Pertinent Robots in Gerontological Healthcare
    Xavier Alameda-Pineda, Angus Addlesee, Daniel Hernández García, and 41 more authors
    In International Journal of Social Robotics, 2025
    Abs

    This paper examines whether autonomous social robots with conversational abilities can be practically useful in elderly care settings. We conducted two experimental phases at a Parisian day-care facility, testing a full-sized humanoid robot with roughly 60 end-users including patients and caregivers. We assessed acceptability using the Acceptability Evaluation Scale and usability via the System Usability Scale. Our findings reveal that users are receptive to this technology, especially when the robot’s perception and action skills are robust to environmental challenges. We also document user concerns and suggestions for improvement in this previously underexplored gerontological healthcare environment.

  2. Building for Speech: Designing the Next Generation of Social Robots for Audio Interaction
    Angus Addlesee and Ioannis Papaioannou
    In Frontiers in Robotics and AI, 2025
    Abs

    Despite significant advances in robotics, conversational AI, and spoken dialogue systems, social robots remain largely absent from public spaces. We argue that early-stage collaboration between robot designers and spoken dialogue system researchers is crucial for creating social robots that can legitimately be used in real-world environments. We identify key challenges including insufficient audio output for noisy environments and hearing-impaired users, inadequate microphone arrays compared to commercial voice assistants, ego-noise from robot motors degrading speech recognition, and fragile joints posing safety risks to vulnerable populations. We recommend fitting multiple directional speakers, high-quality microphone arrays, quieter motors, and more robust joints so that researchers can create spoken dialogue systems that are more accessible and able to handle multi-party conversations in populated environments.

2024

  1. Incremental multi-party conversational AI for people with dementia
    Angus Addlesee
    Heriot-Watt University, 2024
    Abs

    This thesis develops incremental multi-party conversational AI systems designed to support people with dementia. Current voice assistants assume turn-based interaction with a single user, but clinical settings involve multiple participants with varying cognitive abilities. We present systems that process speech incrementally, manage multi-party hospital conversations, and adapt to the speech production changes associated with dementia including extended pauses, interrupted utterances, and word-finding difficulties.

  2. You Have Interrupted Me Again!: Making Voice Assistants More Dementia-Friendly With Incremental Clarification
    Angus Addlesee and Arash Eshghi
    Frontiers in Dementia, 2024
    Abs

    Speakers seldom have a full plan of what they are going to say in advance and require incremental planning during speech, often resulting in pauses. This is particularly important for accessibility, as individuals with dementia experience longer and more frequent pauses, yet current voice assistants interrupt prematurely. We developed SLUICE-CR, a corpus containing 3,000 human-generated incremental clarification requests (iCRs), and tested large language models’ ability to both generate and respond to these requests appropriately. Our findings show that larger LLMs interpret incremental clarificational exchanges more effectively, indicating these models can be configured to handle speech patterns typical of people with dementia and reduce user frustration.

  3. Multi-party Multimodal Conversations Between Patients, Their Companions, and a Social Robot in a Hospital Memory Clinic
    Angus Addlesee, Neeraj Cherakara, Nivan Nelson, and 5 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 18th Conference of the European Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics (EACL), 2024
    EACL Best Demo Award Abs

    true

    We present an LLM-based spoken dialogue system deployed in a hospital setting, embodied by the ARI social robot. The system enables patients and companions to have conversations together with multimodal input and output including speech, video, and gestures. It determines when to participate in conversation, generates human-like clarification requests when patients pause mid-utterance, answers in-domain questions grounded in provided knowledge, and appropriately responds to out-of-domain requests such as jokes and quizzes. The system’s ability to handle unexpected patient utterances represents a significant advancement in real-world deployment of dialogue systems.

  4. Clarifying Completions: Evaluating How LLMs Respond to Incomplete Questions
    Angus Addlesee, Oliver Lemon, and Arash Eshghi
    In Proceedings of the Joint International Conference on Computational Linguistics, Language Resources and Evaluation (LREC-COLING), 2024
    Abs

    Humans process language incrementally, which leads to conversational phenomena like incremental clarification requests (iCRs) when someone pauses mid-sentence. We created SLUICE-CR, a corpus containing 3,000 human-generated iCRs, to evaluate whether large language models can generate appropriate clarifying responses to incomplete questions. We find that the ability to produce contextually suitable iCRs only appears in larger language models and requires prompting with example iCRs from our corpus. The results suggest that autoregressive language models can understand and generate language incrementally, with implications for making conversational AI systems more accessible for users with memory impairment.

  5. A Multi-party Conversational Social Robot Using LLMs
    Angus Addlesee, Neeraj Cherakara, Nivan Nelson, and 6 more authors
    In Companion of the 2024 ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), 2024
    Abs

    We present an LLM-based spoken dialogue system for a social robot deployed in a hospital memory clinic. The system accepts speech and video inputs while generating speech and gestures, manages turn-taking in multi-party conversations, generates clarification requests when patients pause mid-utterance, and handles both in-domain clinical questions and out-of-domain social requests. We demonstrate coordination with clinicians and carers in real hospital settings.

  6. Self-ownership, not self-production, modulates bias and agency over a synthesised voice
    Bryony Payne, Angus Addlesee, Verena Rieser, and 1 more author
    Cognition, 2024
    Abs DOI

    Voices are fundamentally social stimuli, and their importance to the self may be underpinned by how far they can be used to express the self in communication contexts. We created an experimental setup using a two-player online game where participants communicated through text-to-speech technology, then measured participants’ psychological identification with and perceived agency over the synthesized voices. Our key finding is that ownership of the voice matters more than whether participants actively produced or controlled its creation in determining their bias toward it and their sense of agency.

  7. A Holistic Evaluation Methodology for Multi-Party Spoken Conversational Agents
    Nancie Gunson, Angus Addlesee, Daniel Hernández Garcia, and 2 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 24th International Conference on Intelligent Virtual Agents (IVA), 2024
    Abs

    While research has advanced sub-tasks like speaker identification, there is a gap in fully autonomous multi-party applications users can directly interact with. We develop a multi-modal robot receptionist for hospital waiting rooms using Large Language Models and present an experimental methodology and design of task-based user experiments that captures both objective measures of multi-party dialogue performance (such as accurate tracking of user goals) and the users’ subjective experience of multi-party embodied conversations, providing a holistic methodology for the future evaluation of multi-party spoken conversational agents.

  8. Grounding LLMs to In-prompt Instructions: Reducing Hallucinations Caused by Static Pre-training Knowledge
    Angus Addlesee
    In Proceedings of the 3rd workshop on Safety for Conversational AI (Safety4ConvAI), 2024
    Abs

    LLMs can hallucinate when domain-specific knowledge in prompts conflicts with their pre-training data. We curated a QA corpus with information unseen during training and tested various LLMs while manipulating prompt and knowledge formats. We find that a specific prompt format consistently improves the model’s textual grounding to given knowledge, yielding accuracy improvements of up to 28% across healthcare and finance domains with a 12% mean improvement. We also identify that hierarchical and direct node-property graph structures can enhance interpretability and controllability, enabling safer LLM deployment in critical applications.

  1. Multi-party Goal Tracking with LLMs: Comparing Pre-training, Fine-tuning, and Prompt Engineering
    Angus Addlesee, Weronika Sieińska, Nancie Gunson, and 3 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 24th Annual Meeting of the Special Interest Group on Discourse and Dialogue, 2023
    Abs

    We recorded 29 interactions involving patients, companions, and a social robot in a hospital setting, then labeled them for goal-tracking and intent-slot tasks. We compared three approaches: fine-tuning T5, pre-training DialogLM with LED, and prompt engineering with GPT-3.5-turbo using minimal data. GPT-3.5-turbo significantly outperformed the others in a few-shot setting, with reasoning-style prompts achieving the best results at 62.32% goal-tracking accuracy and 69.57% intent-slot accuracy when trained on just 7% of the corpus. Story-style prompts increased hallucinations, posing safety concerns in critical applications.

  4. Understanding Disrupted Sentences Using Underspecified Abstract Meaning Representation
    Angus Addlesee and Marco Damonte
    In Proceedings of INTERSPEECH 2023, 2023
    Abs DOI

    We investigate whether semantic parsing can help voice assistants understand disrupted speech from groups like people with dementia or hearing impairments, as well as handle external noise disruptions. We created datasets pairing disrupted sentences with underspecified Abstract Meaning Representation (AMR) graphs. Our best disruption recovery pipeline lost only 1.6% graph similarity f-score when compared to a model given the full original sentence, demonstrating that semantic parsing techniques can significantly improve spoken dialogue system robustness for both accessibility and noise-handling purposes.

  1. Securely Capturing People’s Interactions with Voice Assistants at Home: A Bespoke Tool for Ethical Data Collection
    Angus Addlesee
    In Proceedings of the Second Workshop on NLP for Positive Impact (NLP4PI), 2022
    Abs

    Speech patterns vary individually, yet current spoken dialogue systems rely on general patterns that do not serve all user groups well, particularly those with conditions like dementia who produce more disfluent speech. We present CVR-SI, a bespoke tool for ethical data collection designed initially for healthcare but applicable more broadly. We explain why commercial solutions fail to protect sensitive data adequately, then detail the security and ethical design of our device, including instructions for building both hardware and software components accessible to researchers without hardware expertise.

  2. The spoon is in the sink: Assisting visually impaired people in the kitchen
    Katie Baker, Amit Parekh, Adrien Fabre, and 3 more authors
    In Proceedings of the Reasoning and Interaction Conference (ReInAct 2021), 2021
    Abs

    We present an extended Visual Question Answering system designed to help blind and partially sighted individuals navigate kitchens, built on three key design principles: privacy (as the camera may capture sensitive items), transparency for explaining system behaviour, and controllability for domain customisation. The enhanced system can address queries about quantity, positioning, and system confidence regarding 299 kitchen objects. Spatial relationship questions prove particularly valuable for visually impaired users, and the system delivers more practical responses than comparable end-to-end VQA technologies.

  3. Incremental Graph-Based Semantics and Reasoning for Conversational AI
    Angus Addlesee and Arash Eshghi
    In Proceedings of the Reasoning and Interaction Conference (ReInAct 2021), 2021
    Abs

    Next-generation conversational AI systems require incremental processing (handling language token-by-token), incremental reasoning (establishing meaning beyond literal utterances), and transparency and control (enabling designers to explain behaviour). We propose combining Dynamic Syntax, an incremental semantic grammar framework, with the Resource Description Framework (RDF) to create incremental semantic parsers that progressively output semantic RDF graphs as an utterance unfolds in real-time. This hybrid approach yields semantic infrastructure that can be used to build responsive, real-time, interpretable conversational AI customisable for specialised populations including people with dementia.

  1. A comprehensive evaluation of incremental speech recognition and diarization for conversational AI
    Angus Addlesee, Yanchao Yu, and Arash Eshghi
    In Proceedings of the 28th International Conference on Computational Linguistics, 2020
    Abs

    We evaluate automatic speech recognition and speaker diarisation systems from major commercial providers for conversational AI applications, assessing how well they handle real-time incremental processing with challenges like speech disfluencies and overlapping dialogue. Microsoft offers the leading incremental ASR system which preserves disfluent materials, IBM excels in speaker diarisation and robustness to overlaps, and Google provides middle-ground performance. However, none of these systems are yet suitable to reliably handle natural spontaneous conversations in real-time, which is critical for dialogue systems handling multi-party interactions.

  2. Ethically Collecting Multi-Modal Spontaneous Conversations with People that have Cognitive Impairments
    Angus Addlesee and Pierre Albert
    In LREC 2020 Workshop Language Resources and Evaluation Conference 11–16 May 2020, 2020
    Abs

    Recordings of multi-modal spontaneous conversations with vulnerable user groups are scarce, and such data collection presents ethical and legal complexities that many researchers lack experience navigating. We consulted with experts over a year-long period and created CUSCO, a secure system for capturing and sharing sensitive recordings. This work provides practical guidance to help researchers ethically collect conversational data from people with cognitive impairments while maintaining appropriate security standards for vulnerable populations.

  1. Current Challenges in Spoken Dialogue Systems and Why They Are Critical for Those Living with Dementia
    Angus Addlesee, Arash Eshghi, and Ioannis Konstas
    In Dialogue for Good (DiGo), 2019
    Abs

    Current dialogue systems like Alexa are not yet naturally interactive: they are often turn-based, have naive end-of-turn detection, and overlook crucial conversational cues including backchannels, hesitation markers, filled pauses, gaze, and disfluencies. This limitation poses particular challenges for vulnerable populations, as dialogue system users in healthcare contexts are likely to be frail, older, distracted, or suffer from cognitive decline which impacts their ability to make effective use of current systems. We propose urgent research priorities including incremental speech recognition development and systematic analysis of conversation patterns potentially diagnostic of dementia.
